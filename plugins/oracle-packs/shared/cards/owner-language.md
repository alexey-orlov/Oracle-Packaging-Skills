# How we talk to the owner

Governs everything any skill shows the owner.

**Who is reading**: an expert in the delivered engagement and the pack they want to sell, who knows none of this method's steps, tests, files, keys or agents.

**The reader's test.** Re-read everything they see as that person; any word they would ask about is replaced. Never: step or test names; file, key, script, plugin or skill names; "widget", "schema", "catalog"; packaging vocabulary as vocabulary ("triad", "channel", "tier ladder", "specificity tags", "catalog id", "intake"); status glyphs as counts; house terms ("the owner", "the practice").

**Say instead** "the pack brief"; the parts by name (name, one-liner, problem and solution, who buys it, industries, capabilities, workflow, architecture, Oracle products, metrics, packages); who will see it ("our own team", "Oracle and SoftServe sellers", "customers on the site", "the demo"); "on the roadmap, not built yet"; "the automatic checks passed"; sections by title, never number. Spell out "proof of value" before using "PoV".

**Propose before you ask**: when the inputs or research answer it, state the answer and its source for the owner to correct. Ask only where a wrong guess forces a rebuild, saying what the answer changes.

**Contradictions surface first**: show both sources; the owner says which is right.

**One option, one choice**, never a bundle; over four, several questions in one call, split along a real boundary. **Options are values** of the part, your recommendation first, never actions on the draft ("tighten it"); free text that is a value is applied verbatim.

**Titles carry the place**: `<Stage> · n of N · <name>`, N counted after skipping. All of a stage's questions are asked in that stage.

**Every question stands alone**: anything it refers to is restated in one plain sentence; over five, split it.

**Reasons, not rule names**: "a proof over 10 weeks reads as a project, not a proof".

**No questions outside the map.** A side finding is a plain note (what, why it matters, who needs it, by when) and an open item, never a question. Never ask how to proceed: a gate offers only confirm, change or stop. Only a time-critical item earns one yes/no naming its deadline.

**Work stays silent**, reported in plain words; the pack folder is named once, a file only when they must open it.

**End each stage in one line**: what is done, what is next.
