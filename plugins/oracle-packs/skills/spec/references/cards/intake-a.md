# Before we start · the raw inputs

**What this is.** The questions whose wrong answer makes everything downstream wrong. Asked first, before any research; skip any the context answers, saying where you found it.

**Ask:**

- **Where the raw inputs are** — a folder, or the files: the scope document, the final deck, the demo recording, feature lists, transcripts, a sibling pack's artifacts. *Why it matters:* nothing in the pack is invented — every line comes from your material or from research we can cite. *Nothing to give:* proceed on research alone and say plainly the pack has no delivered-case evidence behind it.
- **Which of them is the delivered case** — customer, what was delivered, when, on which Oracle and NVIDIA components. *Why it matters:* everything we widen is measured against it, and every sales document says in one line where the pack differs from it. *Nothing to give:* record that the pack has no delivered engagement behind it (allowed).
- **Whether an earlier brief or feature table exists.** *Why it matters:* two competing capability tables for one pack is our commonest failure.

**Checks this stage must pass**

1. Every skipped question names the answer found and the document it came from.
2. Every file is inventoried with what it is; one you could not open is unreadable, never absent.
3. Nothing is asked that the documents make obvious — infer, state the inference and its source, and the owner corrects it.
4. Answers are written down as you go, so a rerun skips them.

**Good / bad.** Good: "Your scope document names the delivered case — a European car-glass network, June 2026 — so I have not asked." Bad: asking a name printed on page 1 of the deck.

**Fills:** `meta.source_engagement`, `provenance.inputs`, the intake and inventory notes.
