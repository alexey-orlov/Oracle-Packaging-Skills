# The industries

**What this is.** Three to five industries the pack is sold into, each carrying its **own** one-line problem and solution, a "what matters here" line, and where it stands. A chip row of industry nouns is the degenerate form, used only where a page has no room.

**Checks the draft must pass**

1. Three to five industries (a sixth usually means two are the same operation), each with a phrase naming **what actually differs there** — the entities, the constraint, the clock. An industry that cannot fill it is the same business under another name and is cut.
2. Each has its own problem and solution line, in business language, one concrete artefact, no product or vendor names — never the pack's line with the industry noun swapped in.
3. "What matters here" is the two or three step-level differences that change the work, ≤ 20 words. The full grid stays in the research summary.
4. Each carries its standing: **proven with a customer** (only the delivered one) · **plausible** (evidence, not delivered) · **not built yet**. Nothing else is called proven.
5. One label per industry, used identically everywhere; the owner's own words for their market win.
6. The first and second priority are the owner's answer from the start of the run, not the research's ranking.

**Good / bad.** Good (telecom): "Install-and-repair technicians have to be routed to tight appointment windows across regions, matched to line skills; missed windows cost customer satisfaction directly." Bad: "Telecom — plan the mobile workforce by hand" (the pack's own line, industry noun swapped).

**Fills:** `verticals[]` in priority order — `name`, `site_label`, `what_matters_here`, `status`; `verticals[].framing` — `problem`, `solution`.
