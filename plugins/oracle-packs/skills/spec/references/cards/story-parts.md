# The one-liner, the buyer and the name

**What this is.** What every story candidate's one-liner, buyer line and name must pass, beside card `story`.

**The one-liner** comes in two lengths: full, for print and hero copy, and short, said in one breath and derived from the full one.

1. It answers three questions in order: of what (the object, in the buyer's terms), so what for the business (money, time or risk), how (one clause, generalized).
2. A qualifier only the delivered customer's circumstance explains is cut.
3. It describes the product, not an artifact ("AI accelerator service packages on Oracle OCI" describes a deck), with no packaging vocabulary ("accelerator pack", "ready-to-run", "packaged"), counts or taxonomy.

**Who buys it**: one line with the buyer's role, the company and the pain, from the customer's side: a shape of operation, not an industry list. The partner's product they run may qualify the line, never define it. No headcount threshold without a source. It reads as the feature list's "For …" line and the site's "Where it applies"; buyer roles, qualifying signals and disqualifiers stay internal.

**The name**: noun-led, the thing and not the job ("Damage assessment", never "Repair or replace"); plain enough that an Oracle rep needs no briefing and a buyer would search for it; no packaging vocabulary, counts, customer name or internal acronym; a vendor trademark in it has its usage rules checked. Each audience's variant and casing is derived once, by rule (`shared/references/naming-and-clearance.md`).

**Good.** "A region's four-week field plan, optimized in minutes and approved by dispatchers." `Workforce Optimization` (site), `Workforce Optimization App` (internal), `Workforce optimization` (print).
**Bad.** "Decide repair or replace from the photos a customer already sends": of what? And the qualifier narrows it to one customer.
