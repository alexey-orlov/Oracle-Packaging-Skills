# When the page runs to two

**What this is.** The one-pager is one A4 page, always. When the render is longer, content is cut — never smaller type, never narrower margins, never a second page. The type scale is the brand.

**Where the numbers come from.** The builder exits 3 and prints every block with its word count and its budget, over-budget first. Read that table; do not re-derive it.

**Checks**

1. The ask reaches the owner in plain words: which block, named as they would name it ("the problem paragraph", "the packages table"), roughly how many words it must lose, and what that costs them.
2. Each option is a cut to choose between, one choice each — never "tighten it up", never a bundle of edits.
3. Peer items stay peers: three problem bullets of 7–12 words, three seller bullets of 13–18, three stat labels of 11–13. A block inside its total still reads wrong when one item is three times its neighbour.
4. The agreed wording is applied through the spec skill's fast path, so the deck, the listing and this page keep saying the same thing. Never edited into the built HTML alone.
5. Rebuild and re-run the page check; the page is one page before anything is shown.
6. When no block is over budget and the page is still long, the cut comes from the longest blocks — the tool ranks them.

**Bad.** "The page is 6 % over — shall I drop the body text to 8 pt?"
**Good.** "It runs to two pages. The proof story is the longest block: about ten words out of it, or one of the three figures, brings it back."

**Writes:** the agreed wording into the spec (`one_pager:` overrides, `problem_solution`, `kpis[]`), never the output file.
