# How we talk to the owner

_Copy of `plugins/oracle-packs/skills/spec/references/cards/owner-language.md`; keep in sync._

Governs every message, title, option, card and table this run produces. Long form, never loaded at run time: `shared/references/talking-to-the-owner.md`.

**Who is reading.** The owner is expert in exactly two things — the delivered engagement and the pack they want to sell. They have not read this method and do not know its steps, tests, files, keys or agents.

**The reader's test.** Re-read anything they will see as that person; any word they would ask about is replaced. Never in owner-facing text: internal step or test names; file names, spec keys, script, plugin or skill names, "widget", "schema", "catalog"; packaging vocabulary as vocabulary ("triad", "channel", "tier ladder", "specificity tags", "catalog id"); ● ◐ ○ as counts; house terms ("the owner", "the practice").

Say instead: the parts of the pack by name (name, one-liner, problem and solution, who buys it, industries, capabilities, workflow, architecture, Oracle products, metrics, packages); "who will see it — our own team, Oracle and SoftServe sellers, customers on the site, the demo"; "three capabilities are on the roadmap, not built yet"; "the automatic checks passed"; sections by their title, never by number.

**Propose before you ask.** A question is the last resort. When the inputs or the research answer it, state the answer with its source and move on; the owner corrects what is wrong. Ask only where a wrong guess forces a rebuild rather than an edit.

**Contradictions surface before the question**: when two inputs disagree, or an answer contradicts a document, show both with their sources and let the owner say which is right.

**One option = one choice**, never a bundle. Over four choices → several questions in one call, split along a real boundary.

**Options are values** of the part — candidate names, candidate one-liners — never actions on the draft ("tighten it", "drop the clause"); free-text "Other" is always there, applied verbatim when it is a value.

**Titles carry the place**: `<Stage> · n of N · <name>`, N counted after skipping.

**Every question stands alone**: anything it refers to is restated in the card in one plain sentence. Over five of those, the question is too big — split it.

**Reasons, not rule names**: "a proof over 10 weeks reads as a project, not a proof".

**End each stage in one line**: what is done, what is next.
