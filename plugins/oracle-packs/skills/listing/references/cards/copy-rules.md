# Writing the copy

**What this is.** How the strings are written once the keys are mapped: whose seat the page speaks from, what a heading costs, what words never appear. Every word lives in the data file — renderers read data and invent nothing.

**The checks**

1. **Persona first, in the reader's words.** Every headline and lead is written from the seat of a partner rep opening the page live on a call, and of a buyer reading alone. Read it aloud as the rep: would they say it to a customer?
2. **No counts, no taxonomy, no packaging vocabulary.** "seven products", "three workflow patterns", "packaged", "ready-to-run", "accelerator pack", "pods", "scoped", "evaluation-first" are internal. A number is a headline only when it is the reader's information — a price, a duration.
3. **Structure before copy.** Audience → how this page differs from its siblings → three or four messages, each answering a reader problem → one screen per message → a length target set up front. Name the messages before writing a sentence, and cut any block whose message you cannot state.
4. **Heading budgets, counted on the rendered page at 375 px, not in the file.** H1 two to four words, ≤ ~24 characters a line, ≤ 2 lines. H2 ≤ 5 words, ≤ ~30 characters. Light-band title ≤ ~28. No lone short word on a line. **The argument moves into the lead.**
5. **Repetition limits.** No content word three times on one screen; one word for one thing throughout; no claim in more than two places. Run a word-frequency pass.
6. **Mechanical pass before it ships.** No `&amp;`-style entities, no retired vocabulary, sentence case and US spelling everywhere (*modeled*, never *modelled*), confirmations truthful: nothing said sent unless something sent it.

Claims and names: `claim-rules`. Altitude: `exemplar-altitude`.
