# What may be claimed

**What this is.** The rules that bite on every string: what may be said about results, statuses, partners and customers. They apply while writing the copy and again at the gates.

**The checks**

1. **No invented facts, numbers, customers or URLs.** Every clause of `oneLiner`, `metrics[]`, `caseStudy` and `jumpstart` maps to a spec line carrying a `source`. An unsupported clause is **dropped**, never swapped for another claim.
2. **Never state a ceiling, a total, a denominator or a negation.** No catalog size, no "N of M", no zero-count facet, no "so far", no "yet". Say what is here, positively, and only where cleared. No sentence whose subject is an absence.
3. **A status is said once, in one plain word** — proven / forecast / estimated / measured / modeled / in preparation — and the footnote spends its line on **evidence**: what the figure is measured or modeled against. Never the negation of the chip's word.
4. **No customer name or logo in anything shipped** — copy, alt text, captions, file names, data files, or unreferenced files under the publish root. Describe a customer by industry and scale, with nothing that back-solves to one company. Anything that must never ship lives **outside** the publish root.
5. **No partner-standing claim** — no tier, no award, no status; joint delivery may be said. **Nothing internal ships**: no internal taxonomy, company, product, file name, path, meeting date, operating number or personal mailbox. The only contact is a named person plus a shared practice mailbox.
6. **Canonical vocabulary.** The platform chip carries the vendor's own product name, verbatim and unabbreviated. **Commitments are enforced, not written**: a duration, a price or a promise is flagged for delivery sign-off, never settled on the builder's say-so.

Prices and tiers: `entry-jumpstart`.
