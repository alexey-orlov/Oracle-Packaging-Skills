# The design — value first

**What this is.** The shape of the walkthrough, confirmed with a widget **before** any code: about six guided steps, the value band, the drill-down, the manual override, the moments where the system is seen reasoning.

**The checks**

1. **Lead with the value, not the screens.** The improvement is the **first thing the viewer sees after the run**: before → after on the pack's own KPIs, in **one band**. A screen tour that buries the gain fails the brief.
2. **Then the drill-down**: from each number to the changes that produced it — **the rule and its effect**, per change. **Then the manual override** that recomputes, so the viewer overrules the machine and watches everything follow.
3. **A dedicated passive step pauses on the improved metrics** — no action asked, a Next control, every other click blocked including its own target's children — and the step after it opens **where the gain comes from**.
4. **The system is seen reasoning at every step.** Heavy work behind a progress bar reads as ETL. The viewer watches it scan every source, rank by business impact, attribute a cause sitting in a different system from the symptom, and recommend actions. Pick a decision **with money on it**; promise an **outcome**, never hours saved. **Open on the integrated sources, not a job run.**
5. **Hint titles are business sentences in the persona's words** — write all six before building. None says refresh, model, mapping, view or SQL outside an explain or trace panel. Hints allow only the designated control; free exploration follows the tour.
6. **The end card is for the viewer**: what they can act on now — three sentences, three doors, **no figures**. Never a recap of what the build did.
