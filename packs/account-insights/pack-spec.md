## Problem and solution

### Problem

An account manager, a partnerships lead or a portfolio manager covers dozens of companies and cannot keep up with the news on them, so rivals reach the openings first.

### Solution

Each morning they review a ready list of the changes that matter across the companies they cover, what each means and the next move, and take the opening before a rival does.

- **Problem points:**
  - **Too much to keep up with:** dozens of companies, hundreds of sources, one person
  - **Found out too late:** the update surfaces after the company has already decided
  - **Only the company in the headline:** what it means for its suppliers, customers, competitors or sister holdings is never worked out
- **Outcome chips:** Coverage ↑; Time to first move ↓; Second-order opportunities surfaced
- **Reframe:** Win the work the news opens
- **Reframe question:** What if the team got there before its rivals?
- **Today:** They read what they can, the night before a meeting, and only about the company in the headline. Its effect on their other companies goes unseen, and rivals get there first.
- **Tomorrow:** Each morning they open a short list: the changes that matter across their companies, what each means and the named next move, with the evidence beside it. They approve, reject or comment, and each approved move enters the pipeline before a rival calls.
- **Note:** Rewritten after Alex called the first version "waterish" (2026-09-22). The original named an abstract activity — "commercial teams work out what a development means for their accounts by hand" — instead of the role and the concrete things piling up on that person. Test to apply: could an account manager say this sentence about their own week? Widened 2026-09-22 from the account-manager seat alone to the three seats the same job has.

## Who buys it

Teams keeping up with the companies they cover — customers, partners, holdings.

- **Buyer roles:** Chief Commercial Officer; VP Sales; Head of Key Accounts; Head of Partnerships; Head of Business Development; Portfolio Operations Lead; Head of Sales Operations
- **Qualifying signals:**
  - A named, finite set of companies the team is responsible for — accounts, partner prospects or holdings — owned by people, not a total addressable market
  - A real catalog of moves the team can make in response — services to sell, partnership offers to propose, value-creation levers to pull
  - Research on those companies is done today by the people who own the relationship, not by a central research function
  - A system of record holding first-party context worth grounding against — a CRM, a deal pipeline, a portfolio tracker
  - Companies whose own suppliers, customers, competitors or sister holdings are also in the set — this is what makes ripple reasoning pay
- **Disqualifiers:**
  - No catalog of moves to map to — a fund screening deal targets has nothing to map an implication onto
  - Open, undiscovered universes: the pack resolves signals against a roster it is given; it does not go and find new names
  - Teams wanting an action taken automatically — the pack informs decisions, it does not act on them
  - Teams already standardized on a first-party-only intelligence tool with no appetite for external signal
- **Note:** Stated as a shape across three seats — sales, partnerships, portfolio — so it holds across the industries. The last qualifying signal is the differentiator restated as a qualification question.

## Workflow

### Inputs

| System | Data | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| The team's system of record — CRM, deal pipeline or portfolio tracker | the in-scope companies, their framing and relationship notes, the owner of each | PoV Jumpstart: an agreed extract. Integration: API. |
| External signal sources | news, regulatory filings, disclosures, commercial data feeds | PoV Jumpstart: a bounded, licence-cleared source set. Integration: the client's own licensed feeds. |
| The organization's catalog of moves | service lines, partnership offers or value-creation levers, captured from the organization's own material | PoV Jumpstart: captured once. Integration: maintained against a versioned catalog. |

### 1. Set the scope

- **Actor:** human
- **Human in the loop:** yes
- **Covers:** the in-scope companies and their owners, the signal sources, and the organization's own catalog of moves — services, partnership offers, value levers
- **Description:** The client names the accounts that are in scope and who owns each, agrees the signal sources and their licensing, and hands over the catalog of moves an implication can be mapped onto.
- **If it fails:** A company with no framing in the system of record is admitted and flagged as thinly grounded, never silently dropped. A source that cannot be licensed for model use and redistribution is excluded at this step rather than discovered at procurement.

### 2. Pick up the signal

- **Actor:** system
- **Covers:** ingestion, relevance filtering, de-duplication, account resolution, fan-out
- **Description:** Signals arrive on a schedule, on demand for one account, or by manual submit. Each is judged genuine, de-duplicated across sources into one signal, and resolved to the in-scope accounts it actually concerns — one record per affected account.
- **If it fails:** A name matching two accounts or none goes to human triage, never a guess. An unreachable or paywalled source is recorded as unreachable, never as "nothing found" — a feed that is down must not read as a quiet week.

### 3. Work out what it means

- **Actor:** system
- **Covers:** evidence assembly and ranking, the "so what" per account, second-order ripples
- **Description:** Evidence is assembled and ranked from first-party and public sources, the implication is reasoned for that account as opportunities and risks, and the effect is followed to the account's suppliers, customers and competitors that are themselves in the book.
- **If it fails:** NOT COVERED, by this pack or by any product in this market — citation verification proves the quote is real, nothing proves the business conclusion follows from it. The reviewer at step 5 is the only check, which is why that step is not optional. Beyond two levels, or with no relationship data, a ripple is stated as unknown rather than asserted.

### 4. Map it to your next move

- **Actor:** system
- **Covers:** mapping to a service line, a partnership offer or a value lever — and the demand gap when nothing fits
- **Description:** Each implication is mapped to a named move the organization can actually make — a service to offer, a partnership to propose, a lever to pull — so the output is something to go and do rather than something to read.
- **If it fails:** An implication that maps to nothing is emitted as mapped-to-nothing — never forced onto the nearest catalog entry. Where a seat or vertical has no catalog the step is switched off rather than faked.

### 5. Score it, evidence it, review it

- **Actor:** human
- **Human in the loop:** yes
- **Covers:** magnitude and confidence, citations, the reviewer's approve / reject with a reason
- **Description:** Each item carries a magnitude, a confidence and its source evidence; the reviewer reads the short list and approves, rejects or comments on why something is or is not pursuable.
- **If it fails:** Below the confidence threshold an item is filtered rather than delivered, and the filtered count is reported so silence stays legible. A rejection is retained with its reason, never deleted. Contradiction between reliable sources is currently resolved by rank order rather than surfaced as a finding — roadmap.

### 6. Deliver it and learn from it

- **Actor:** system
- **Covers:** routing to the owner, export to the system of record, outcome capture
- **Description:** Approved records reach the person who owns that account, in the system they work in, and what became of each one feeds back into scoring.
- **If it fails:** In the proof of value nothing is written back — reviewer decisions are captured, not persisted. Routing is Integration tier; misrouting is the most common way a correct implication produces no action. Outcome capture is Scaling tier, and without it the confidence score can only ever be asserted, never calibrated.

| System | Data | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| The team's system of record | approved per-account records — account, trigger, impact reasoning, scored opportunities with next-move mapping and cross-account effects | PoV Jumpstart: file export / download. Integration: API export. Scaling: live write-back with idempotency. |
