# Component 2 — the one-liner

**What this is.** One sentence stating the job and the outcome in the reader's words, carried in two lengths: a **full** form for print and hero copy, and a **short** form a partner rep can say in one breath on a call. The reader of record is the Oracle or SoftServe seller who has never worked in the industry.

**Checks the draft must pass**

1. It answers **three questions, in order**: of what — the object the job is about, named in the buyer's terms; so what for the business — the outcome in money, time or risk; how — in one clause, generalized.
2. Every qualifier survives the test *"does the claim hold if this customer's circumstance differs?"* If it does, the qualifier goes ("the photos a customer already sends", "the rule that applies in that country").
3. No packaging vocabulary. "Accelerator pack", "ready-to-run", "packaged", "pods", "evaluation-first" are internal words and never reach a reader. No counts, no taxonomy.
4. It describes the **product**, not the artifact — "AI accelerator service packages on Oracle OCI" describes a deck.
5. The short form is a derivation of the full one, not a third sentence.

**Good.** Short: *"A region's four-week field plan, optimized in minutes and approved by dispatchers."* — the unit of work, the outcome, and who stays in charge. Full: *"Intelligent field-service planning with NVIDIA cuOpt on Oracle OCI: packaged from proof of value to enterprise scale."*

**Bad.** *"Decide repair or replace from the photos a customer already sends"* — of what? and the qualifier narrows the claim to one customer's current situation.

**Fills:** `one_liner.full`, `one_liner.short`, and the site's `heroCaption` derived from them.
